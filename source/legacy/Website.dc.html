01 / Sound familiar?

The ambition is there. The engine isn't keeping up.

Maybe you've just raised. Maybe you're pushing into a new market, launching a new product, or the board wants the next stage of growth. Either way, the revenue has to follow.

You're automating, but nothing is moving.

New AI tools, new sequences, new agents, and the same results. Automation scales what you already do, good or bad.

Growth has stalled.

Headcount went up, the plan looked good, and the numbers didn't follow.

Inbound is drying up.

The leads that used to arrive on their own have slowed, and there's no outbound motion to replace them.

Good leads go cold.

Demo requests wait a day for a reply and get a template.

Sales still runs through one person.

The founder or CEO is in every big deal, and the ICP is “anyone who'll buy”.

Pricing is a negotiation every time.

Too many options and nobody sure what a deal is worth.

A launch needs a new motion.

A new product, market or country, and the old way of selling won't carry it.

Deals take forever.

Long cycles, slipping close dates and a forecast nobody trusts.

02 / The Commercial Engine

Every sales engine runs on one formula.

Track one

Stop the bleeding.

The quick fixes that recover revenue you are already losing: slow replies, stalled deals, discounting, bad-fit leads. Weeks, not quarters.

Track two

Build the future.

The engine you scale on: the right customer, offer, price, pitch, team, process, data and AI workflows. One to three quarters.

How it works
  1. i.

    Diagnose.

    I interview your team and customers, read your CRM and calls, and score your engine across eight areas.

  2. ii.

    Design.

    Who to sell to, what to say, what to charge, the process, the numbers and the AI workflows that make it fast.

  3. iii.

    Build.

    Into the CRM, the team's week and the forecast. Then I hand it over so it runs without me.

We don't fix everything at once. We fix what moves the numbers first.

03 / Case studies

Four companies. Same formula.

The rebuild · 2026

UXCam

AI product analytics · Backed by Headline · Head of Sales, a six-month engagement

~25% → ~40%inbound leads becoming sales-qualified

Before

Inbound was saturating and there was no outbound. The ICP was unclear, the pipeline was bloated, deals stalled after the demo, pricing was complicated, and a new AI agent product was about to launch.

What I did
  • Audited the whole engine in my first weeks and split the fixes into “stop the bleeding” and “build the future”.
  • Reset the ICP and the story for the AI launch.
  • Built a new pricing strategy across every tier, from enterprise down to small businesses.
  • Put AI agents on inbound: every lead scored, enriched and sent a personalised reply within 5 minutes.
  • Built outbound from zero with Clay and AI-personalised sequences.
  • Owned the forecast, and set the quotas and performance framework for the team across five regions.
After

Inbound leads becoming sales-qualified went from ~25% to ~40%. First reply went from about a day to 5 minutes.

The scale · 2024–25

Arbonics

Forest carbon · Backed by Plural · Head of Sales & New Markets, through my consultancy

37×quarterly sales growth in nine months

Before

Three generalist salespeople chasing small landowners from Facebook ads. Long contracts nobody wanted to sign. A CRM held together by broken integrations.

What I did
  • Refocused on professional landowners, and moved from ads to outbound.
  • Split every market into an SDR who creates pipeline and an AE who closes.
  • Sold on return on investment, with a simple first contract and a deeper one later.
  • Moved from Pipedrive to HubSpot: one source of truth for marketing and sales.
  • Built a new cross-market enterprise function selling to funds.
  • Grew the team from 2 to 12 and launched new markets and products with local partners.
After

I led the sales function as quarterly sales grew 37× in nine months: about €10M in estimated contract value won in 12 months, on the company's own model. The average sales cycle fell from 75 to 16 days. 85% of everything Arbonics had ever sold was won while I led sales.

The scale · 2019–21

Zero to one · 2015–16

15-minute healthy food delivery, London · Backed by Playfair Capital and Seedcamp · Head of B2B Sales & Partnerships

0 → 60%of company revenue from B2B

Before

07 / About

I go where things are being built.

I started in the big machine. IBM, Credit Suisse, where I was rated AAA (Exceptional), and Lloyds Banking Group, where I helped create TSB Bank with 5 million customers on day one. Then the agency side, running the UK's biggest rebrand at 23. It was great training in discipline. But I was a cog, and I wanted to feel the impact of my work.

So I jumped. As Entrepreneur in Residence at Oxygen Accelerator, I helped 14 start-ups grow from the ground up, and fell in love with building. I went to San Francisco looking for the next big idea, came back to London with a plan for a food delivery start-up, and then joined Pronto instead: they were a few steps ahead and full of brilliant people. I built their B2B business from nothing.

Then I learned the hard way. I founded my own company, Courage Global, with a co-founder. We spent months perfecting the website, the brand and the concept, and far too little time in front of customers. It didn't work. The lesson stuck: whether it's a start-up, a new product or a new country, the idea comes from you, but the market tells you if it works. Get in front of buyers early, listen hard, and build around what they actually buy.

Since then, I've built engines. Wheely through Covid. Arbonics across four markets. UXCam for the age of AI. The common thread isn't sales. It's taking something new to market and building the engine that keeps it growing: the right customer, the right price, the right pitch, and a process a team can run without me.

Who I work best with: Fast-growing tech companies that make people's lives better: fintech that helps people invest and own more, AI that makes skilled work faster, health, climate and mobility. Well funded, moving fast and serious about using AI properly. I work across the UK, Europe and the US.

08 / FAQ

Questions people ask

Who is Harry James Coburn?
Harry James Coburn is a Berlin-based commercial strategy and go-to-market adviser for growing tech companies. He has led sales at UXCam, Arbonics, Wheely and Pronto, and trained at IBM, Credit Suisse and Lloyds Banking Group.
What does a go-to-market (GTM) adviser do?
A GTM adviser works out who a company should sell to, what to offer, how to price it and how the sales process should run, then helps the team build and run it. I do this hands-on, from diagnosis to handover.
What is a GTM Diagnostic?
A two-to-three-week review of a company's whole sales engine: interviews with the team and customers, a review of the CRM, calls and numbers, a scorecard across eight areas, and a prioritised plan for the next one to three quarters.
What is a GTM Teardown?
A 90-minute working session, prepared from public information about your website, pricing, pitch and funnel. You leave with a one-page list of what to fix first.
What is a fractional Head of Sales?
An experienced sales leader who runs your sales team and process part-time, usually two days a week, while you hire a permanent leader. I also help you hire them.
Who do you work with?
CEOs, founders and commercial leaders of VC-backed tech companies, usually Seed to Series C: B2B SaaS and AI, fintech, health, climate and mobility, across the UK, Europe and the US.
When should a company bring you in?
After a raise, when growth stalls, before launching a new product or market, when sales still depends on the founder, or when AI tools aren't moving the numbers.
How is AI changing B2B sales?
AI agents can now reply to every inbound lead within minutes, research and personalise outbound, and keep reps focused on the deals that matter. But AI scales whatever process you give it, so the strategy, ICP, pricing and pitch have to be right first.
What results have you delivered?
At Arbonics, about €10M in estimated contract value won in 12 months and a sales cycle cut from 75 to 16 days. At UXCam, inbound-to-qualified conversion from ~25% to ~40%. At Pronto, B2B built from zero to 60% of company revenue.
Where are you based?
Berlin, working in person in Berlin and London and remotely across Europe and the US.
Can you join us full-time?
Occasionally, as an interim or permanent Head of Sales, VP Sales or CRO, when the job is to build the engine.
